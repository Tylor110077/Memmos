# Art Direction

## Purpose

Use this file when the hardest part is inventing a visual world that feels specific, memorable, and appropriate to the product instead of merely "modern."

## Build The Design Thesis

Write a short thesis before touching layout details.

- State the audience and emotional target.
- State the functional reality.
- State the visual tension that makes the work memorable.

Formula:

`<mood or cultural cue> + <functional constraint> + <one surprising contrast>`

Examples:

- Quiet editorial intelligence for a research tool, softened with tactile paper-like warmth.
- Brutalist sports energy for an event page, balanced by extremely legible scheduling and ticket flow.
- Museum-label restraint for a portfolio, interrupted by cinematic imagery and dense serif headlines.

## Gather Better Source Material

Avoid copying screenshots of other SaaS products. Pull references from disciplines with stronger visual authorship.

- Editorial design and magazine covers
- Poster systems and exhibition graphics
- Film titles and motion graphics
- Album covers and packaging
- Architecture and interior materials
- Fashion lookbooks and runway staging
- Signage and wayfinding systems
- Book design and grid experiments
- Game UI, maps, control rooms, instruments
- Scientific illustration, diagrams, and archival material

Extract mechanisms, not surfaces:

- Contrast model
- Framing strategy
- Density and whitespace rhythm
- Material cues
- Type hierarchy
- Image cropping logic
- Motion tempo

## Choose A Direction

Commit to one primary direction and one supporting influence at most.

Primary direction examples:

- Editorial
- Cinematic
- Museum/archive
- Neo-industrial
- Playful toy-like
- Nightlife/poster
- Scientific instrument
- Luxury craft
- Retro-futurist
- Monastic minimalism

Supporting influence examples:

- Hand-cut collage
- Transit signage
- Swiss grid discipline
- Analog scan texture
- Technical blueprint lines
- Stage-light glow

If more than two influences are required to explain the concept, the concept is probably weak.

## Theme Axes

Use these axes to keep decisions coherent:

- Formal <-> raw
- Warm <-> cold
- Dense <-> airy
- Static <-> kinetic
- Symmetric <-> off-balance
- Glossy <-> matte
- Precise <-> handmade
- Quiet <-> loud

Pick one side of each axis intentionally. Mixed signals are acceptable only when they create the core tension.

## Resource Strategy

If the design needs supporting visuals, define the role before sourcing them.

- Photography for atmosphere or credibility
- Illustration for concept and tone
- Texture for depth and tactility
- Patterns for section identity
- Icons for navigation clarity
- Diagrams for product explanation

Specify what to source:

- Subject matter
- Framing/crop
- Lighting
- Color bias
- Surface/material quality
- Level of realism

## Anti-Patterns

- Mixing luxury, playful, futuristic, and corporate cues without hierarchy
- Using gradients as a substitute for art direction
- Adding texture everywhere instead of only where it changes the reading experience
- Borrowing a trend without adapting it to the product's actual content
- Building a "beautiful" hero that leaves the rest of the page visually generic
